### Overview
What makes one street more desirable than another when choosing a place to live? This project analyzes how attractive street-level environments are to residents across Rotterdam, revealing what people truly value in their neighborhoods. By combining advanced artificial intelligence with over 200,000 street-level images, researchers mapped the desirability of different areas based on everyday visual features like greenery, buildings, and vehicles.

### The challenge
Urban planners often rely on high-level census data that ignores the day-to-day visual reality of a street. Without detailed, context-specific data on how street-level conditions affect residential attractiveness, cities struggle to design targeted policies. For a diverse and rapidly changing city like Rotterdam, this knowledge gap makes it difficult to decide whether a specific neighborhood needs more trees, fewer parked cars, or more open views to tangibly enhance livability.

### Objectives
- **Map neighborhood desirability** across Rotterdam based on street-level visual conditions and actual resident preferences.
- **Identify specific visual elements** (such as trees, parked cars, or electrical transformer houses) that positively or negatively influence residential choices.
- **Demonstrate the power of flexible AI vision models** to extract fine-grained, localized visual data from street-level imagery for practical urban planning.

### Approach & methodology
To understand what residents value, the research began with an experiment where participants made trade-offs between housing costs, commute times, and street-level views. The team then deployed advanced natural language and vision AI models to analyze over 200,000 Google Street View images of Rotterdam. These models identified common elements like trees and cars, as well as highly specific objects like transformer houses. Finally, they applied their choice model to these segmented images to predict and map the visual attractiveness of streets across the entire city.

### Key findings
- **Attractiveness is highly localized:** Street-level desirability varies rapidly even within small areas. The typically upscale Northern part of Rotterdam has pockets of unappealing street conditions, while some Southern neighborhoods—often perceived as "problem areas"—feature surprisingly attractive visual environments.
- **Vegetation type matters:** People value different types of greenery differently. Trees and plants contribute significantly more to a neighborhood's visual attractiveness than just grass.
- **City center premiums aren't about the view:** The high real-estate prices in Rotterdam's city center are not driven by attractive street-level conditions, which were found to be below average. Instead, they are driven by factors like connectivity and proximity to amenities.
- **Specific infrastructure hurts desirability:** Noticeable utilitarian structures, like electrical transformer houses, tangibly decrease the appeal of a street view.

### Impact & value for the city
These insights provide the Municipality of Rotterdam with a highly detailed, street-by-street map of residential attractiveness based on real human preferences. Urban planners can use this data to make targeted, neighborhood-specific interventions, such as pinpointing exactly where replacing parking spaces with trees will yield the highest increase in local livability.
