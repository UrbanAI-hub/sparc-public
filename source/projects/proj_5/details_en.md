### Overview
Piles of trash abandoned next to underground containers do more than just clutter Rotterdam's streets—they act as magnets for more waste. By combining machine learning risk prediction with routing optimization, this project transforms municipal cleaning services from a reactive force into a proactive, intelligence-led operation. Discover how data-driven dispatching can stop chronic waste hotspots before they even form.

### The Challenge
Illegal dumping, or "naastplaatsing," is a stubborn urban problem where perpetrators hold the power but face few consequences, leaving the city to clean up the mess. Abandoned waste triggers a damaging feedback loop, attracting even more trash and quickly turning isolated incidents into chronic hotspots. Municipal cleaning teams often struggle to balance service quality with operational costs because they are forced to respond blindly to incidents, limiting their overall impact on city cleanliness.

### Objectives
* **Understand** the underlying environmental and operational drivers of illegal waste dumping.
* **Develop** machine learning models that predict dumping risks at the individual container-cluster level.
* **Optimize** the daily routes of municipal cleaning fleets using these predictive algorithms to maximize efficiency.

### Approach
The project started on the streets, combining field experience from Rotterdam's "NietRnaast" cleaning teams with data analysis to uncover the exact triggers for illegal dumping. Using this foundation, researchers trained machine learning models to forecast the specific risk of waste buildup for every individual container. The team then simulated five different routing strategies to find the most efficient dispatch method for the cleaning trucks. This approach acts like a smart GPS, guiding fleets to balance the shortest driving distance with the most urgent cleanup needs.

### Key Findings
* **Smarter routes yield cleaner streets:** A hybrid routing approach that balances driving distance with the risk and visibility of waste significantly outperformed the city's current routes, achieving better cleanliness with less driving.
* **Past behavior predicts future problems:** Historical container behavior emerged as the strongest predictor of future issues, confirming that illegal dumping is a highly patterned and self-reinforcing habit.
* **Functionality drives behavior:** Container malfunctions and overflow incidents are strongly correlated with an immediate increase in illegal dumping.

### Impact
The Municipality of Rotterdam can directly integrate these predictive models and smart routing heuristics into their daily cleaning operations. By rapidly clearing high-visibility hotspots, the city can neutralize the visual multiplier effect that attracts more trash. Conversely, intentionally delaying collection at chronic, low-visibility spots reduces convenience for repeat offenders, empowering Rotterdam to maximize its logistical resources and actively shape citizen behavior.
