### Overview
This project leverages street-level imagery and advanced computer vision to analyze what makes a cycling route attractive in Rotterdam. By combining real-world urban visuals with cyclist preference surveys, the research builds predictive models to pinpoint the exact environmental and infrastructural elements that encourage people to bike.

### The challenge
Designing high-quality cycling infrastructure is crucial for promoting sustainable transport, improving safety, and reducing emissions in our cities. However, urban planners often struggle to quantify how cyclists value different street features—like surface quality, traffic volume, or greenery—and what trade-offs they are willing to make. Without a clear understanding of these preferences, it becomes difficult to design effective infrastructure upgrades that truly resonate with everyday riders.

### Objectives
* **Examine cyclist preferences** regarding specific infrastructural and environmental attributes, such as lane separation, pavement quality, and greenery.
* **Develop novel predictive models** using computer vision that learn directly from real-world visual features instead of traditional map data.
* **Map the alignment** of Rotterdam's current cycling network against actual rider preferences.
* **Simulate and quantify** the impact of targeted infrastructure design changes on a route's overall attractiveness.

### Approach & methodology
Researchers designed an innovative survey where participants chose between hypothetical cycling routes. Instead of reading text descriptions, respondents evaluated thousands of real-world street-level images taken from a cyclist's perspective across Rotterdam. These choices were analyzed alongside travel time and traffic light variables. Using a cutting-edge computer vision model, the system automatically extracted and learned from the visual features in the images, capturing the nuanced realities of the street environment better than traditional methods.

### Key findings
* **Physical separation is paramount:** Cyclists strongly prefer separated cycling lanes and green spaces over dense urban or industrial areas with mixed traffic.
* **Time trade-offs for safety:** On an 11-minute trip, cyclists are willing to add about 1.5 minutes to their journey just to use a separated lane, and almost 1 minute to ride on a completely car-free road.
* **Surface matters:** Smooth asphalt surfaces and red-colored cycling lanes significantly increase the perceived attractiveness of a route compared to standard grey pavement.
* **Paint isn't enough:** Subtle details like painted suggestion lanes have a negligible impact on rider preference when compared to the value of physical separation and high-quality surfaces.

### Impact & value for the city
These new models empower the Municipality of Rotterdam to map exactly which parts of their cycling network are performing well and which segments need urgent improvement based on real user preferences. Urban planners can now use this tool to simulate proposed infrastructure changes—like adding a red asphalt lane or removing car traffic—allowing them to evaluate the prospective impact and effectiveness before investing public funds.
